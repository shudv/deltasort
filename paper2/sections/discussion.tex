\section{Discussion and Limitations}
\label{sec:disc}

\paragraph{Tail latency.}
For most cells the $p99/p50$ ratio is below $5\times$, comfortable for
real-time use. Three cells, however, exhibit substantially higher tails:
A$^*$ ($n{=}10{,}000$, $k{=}5$) at $10.7\times$, tracking
($n{=}50{,}000$, $k{=}5$, clustered) at $12.6\times$, and tracking
($n{=}10{,}000$, $k{=}50$, uniform) at $\mathbf{92\times}$ (median
$9.5~\mu$s, $p99$ $879~\mu$s). A real-time system must validate
$p99$ on its workload before relying on \DeltaSort. The most likely
cause is comparator branch-prediction interaction with cascading insertion
shifts, but a careful isolation experiment (CPU pinning, frequency lock,
P-cores only) is required to confirm.

\paragraph{When to use \DeltaSort.}
The empirical evidence and theory both agree on the operating box:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item The consumer needs the full sorted order, not just the extremum.
  \item Update batches satisfy $k/n \lesssim 5\%$
        (extending to ${\sim}10\%$ when the comparator is expensive).
  \item Dirty indices are known --- either provided by the application
        layer, or cheaply derivable from a write log.
  \item Maintaining a tree's per-node overhead is undesirable (because
        $n$ is small enough that an array fits in cache, or because
        downstream consumers also benefit from contiguous storage).
\end{enumerate}
All seven of our positive workloads tick all four boxes.

\paragraph{Threats to validity.}
(a)~All measurements are single-machine (Apple M3 Pro); we have not
characterized x86 cache and branch behavior. (b)~Synthetic inputs use
seeded RNGs; production traces may exhibit different update locality.
(c)~The harness measures the sort operation in isolation; embedding cost
(re-establishing application-layer pointers into the sorted array) is
application-dependent and not modeled.

\paragraph{Methodology note.}
The verification clause in our harness asserts the strategy's output against
a reference full-sort on every measured cell. This caught a real
\texttt{Ord}/\texttt{PartialOrd} contract violation in two of our application
structs (the lexicographic derive disagreed with the manually-implemented
\texttt{Ord}) that was silent in earlier single-run experiments. We
recommend this practice for any sorting benchmark.
